\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\paragraph{What the theory establishes.}
The marginal spectrum alone does not determine a learning curve for persistent
data.  Two streams can share the same one-time covariance, target, nominal
sample count, and even trace integrated autocorrelation, yet have different
exponents.  In spectral renewal regression the decisive object is the
innovation spectrum $\lambda_j/\tau_j$.  Uniform persistence produces a
constant horizontal shift, whereas persistence increasing toward weak modes
moves the model--data and compute-optimal frontiers.  Stationary heavy dwell
times create a second, separately identifiable phase through forward residual
life.

The results also clarify the role of data reuse.  More epochs can reduce
optimization error on modes present in the dataset, as in existing multi-pass
scaling laws, but they cannot reveal a missing sign in the minimax construction.
The relevant intervention is therefore not always more computation on the same
trajectory.  It may be additional temporal, environmental, or population
diversity that increases the innovation rate of late predictive modes.

\paragraph{What is not claimed.}
The theorem does not state that every dependent Gaussian design has exponent
$(b-1)/(a+r)$.  The dense AR control shows why: when every sample contains every
mode, temporal correlation need not create an occupancy bottleneck.  General
processes may combine at least three effects--innovation coverage,
sample-covariance concentration, and optimization under dependent gradients.
Characterizing their interaction for arbitrary noncommuting space--time
operators remains open.

Representation invariance assumes that the dense map is known, while the
ambient minimum-norm extension is empirical.  The noisy raw-horizon theorem is
sharp in the hard-target regime but does not give a matching minimax result for
all smooth targets.  The equilibrium theorem uses deterministic dwell lengths;
geometric or generally random durations preserve the main intuition but can
change constants and inverse-run-length corrections.  Finally, the real-data
proxy estimates $\tau_j$ after PCA, treats the estimated spectral basis as
fixed, and evaluates one dataset.  A broader benchmark with uncertainty-aware
mode matching is needed before the diagnostic can be treated as universal.

\paragraph{Practical diagnostic.}
The analysis suggests a concrete audit for sequential training data.  Estimate
(i) marginal spectral mass $\widehat\lambda_j$, (ii) task energy
$\widehat s_j$, and (iii) mode-wise refresh or autocorrelation time
$\widehat\tau_j$.  Compare learning-curve forecasts based on
$\widehat\lambda_j$ with those based on
$\widehat\lambda_j/\widehat\tau_j$, and include a randomized-order control.
A persistence correction that helps contiguous windows but not shuffled data
is evidence for a temporal information bottleneck.  Conversely, if dense
sampling already excites all relevant modes, a global correlation correction
or covariance-aware ridge theory may be more appropriate.

\paragraph{Broader implication.}
A scalar effective sample size answers ``how many independent observations is
this trajectory worth on average?''  Scaling laws require the more directional
question ``which predictive modes have received independent information?''
When late modes arrive in long bursts, collecting a longer contiguous log can
be less valuable than increasing coverage, even if both interventions add the
same number of rows to a dataset.
