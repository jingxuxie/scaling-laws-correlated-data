\section{Spectral Renewal Regression}

\paragraph{Marginal prediction problem.}
Let $(e_j)_{j\ge1}$ be an orthonormal spectral basis.  A fresh test example is
\begin{equation}
X=Se_J,
\qquad \Prob(J=j)=\lambda_j,
\qquad \Prob(S=\pm1)=\tfrac12,
\label{eq:test-law}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_j>0$ and $\sum_j\lambda_j=1$.  For a target
$\theta=(\theta_j)_j$, the label is $Y=\langle X,\theta\rangle+\eps$.
The population excess prediction risk is diagonal in this basis:
\begin{equation}
\risk(\widehat\theta,\theta)
=\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_j(\widehat\theta_j-\theta_j)^2,
\qquad s_j:=\lambda_j\theta_j^2.
\label{eq:risk-source}
\end{equation}
The quantity $s_j$ is the predictive energy carried by mode $j$.  Importantly,
all temporal processes considered below have this same one-time test law.

\paragraph{Persistent training stream.}
Associate mode $j$ with a dwell weight $\tau_j\ge1$.  Define
\begin{equation}
Z:=\sum_j\frac{\lambda_j}{\tau_j},
\qquad
q_j:=\frac{\lambda_j/\tau_j}{Z},
\qquad
\mu:=Z^{-1}.
\label{eq:q-def}
\end{equation}
Training is first described at renewal times.  For each of $B$ independent
innovation blocks, draw a mode $J_k\sim q$, draw a Rademacher sign $S_k$, and
observe the block average
\begin{align}
\overline Y_k&=S_k\theta_{J_k}+\overline\eps_k,
&\E[\overline\eps_k\mid J_k=j]&=0,\nonumber\\
&&\E[\overline\eps_k^2\mid J_k=j]&=\sigma^2/\tau_j.
\label{eq:block-observation}
\end{align}
For integer $\tau_j$, this is exactly the average of $\tau_j$ repeated labels
with independent variance-$\sigma^2$ noise.  Expanding every block into its raw
observations gives mean block length
$\sum_jq_j\tau_j=\mu$ and time occupancy
\begin{equation}
\frac{q_j\tau_j}{\mu}=\lambda_j.
\label{eq:occupancy-main}
\end{equation}
Thus changing $\tau$ reorganizes the stream temporally without changing its
marginal covariance.  The number $B$ counts statistically new refreshes; the
raw observation count $N$ is analyzed separately in the raw-trajectory
analysis below.

Equation~\eqref{eq:q-def} is the central modeling constraint.  If a direction
occupies a fixed fraction $\lambda_j$ of time but each visit lasts longer, then
new visits to that direction must occur less often.  Uniform dwell times give
$q=\lambda$ up to normalization.  Mode-dependent dwell times tilt the
innovation law toward rapidly refreshing directions.

\paragraph{Estimator.}
Let
$K_j=\sum_{k\le B}\1\{J_k=j\}$ be the number of blocks in mode $j$.  A model of
size $M$ retains the first $M$ modes and uses the sufficient-statistic average
\begin{equation}
\widehat\theta_j=
\begin{cases}
K_j^{-1}\sum_{k:J_k=j}S_k\overline Y_k,&K_j>0,\\
0,&K_j=0,
\end{cases}
\quad j\le M,
\label{eq:estimator}
\end{equation}
with $\widehat\theta_j=0$ for $j>M$.  The estimator can be implemented online
with one count and one running mean per represented mode.  In the noiseless
case, a single visit reveals a coordinate exactly; all remaining error comes
from model truncation or modes that never arrived.  This makes the information
bottleneck transparent and permits an exact minimax characterization.

\paragraph{Power-law instance.}
Our principal asymptotic regime assumes
\begin{equation}
\lambda_j\asympconst j^{-a},
\qquad
\tau_j\asympconst j^r,
\qquad
s_j\asympconst j^{-b},
\label{eq:power-laws}
\end{equation}
with $a>1$, $r\ge0$, and $b>1$.  The marginal exponent $a$ describes the task
geometry, $r$ describes spectral persistence, and $b$ describes target
regularity.  From Equation~\eqref{eq:q-def},
$q_j\asymp j^{-p}$ with the \emph{persistence-adjusted exponent}
\begin{equation}
p:=a+r.
\label{eq:p-definition}
\end{equation}
The theory below is stated with two-sided regular variation, not only exact
Zipf normalizations.

\paragraph{Dense observed features.}
The coordinate notation is a latent basis, not a requirement that observed
vectors be sparse.  Let $V\in\R^{D\times D}$ be invertible with rows
$v_j^\top$, and observe $X=Sv_J$.  Labels are $S\theta_J$, so the ambient
teacher is $w^\star=V^{-1}\theta$.

\begin{proposition}[Representation invariance]
\label{prop:dense}
For every latent estimate $\widehat\theta$, the ambient estimate
$\widehat w=V^{-1}\widehat\theta$ has exactly the same risk:
\begin{equation}
\E\langle X,\widehat w-w^\star\rangle^2
=\sum_{j=1}^D\lambda_j(\widehat\theta_j-\theta_j)^2.
\label{eq:dense-risk}
\end{equation}
If $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_D$ and
$mI\preceq VV^\top\preceq LI$, the ordered eigenvalues of
$\Sigma=V^\top\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)V$ obey
$m\lambda_j\le\lambda_j(\Sigma)\le L\lambda_j$.
\end{proposition}
The proof is a change of coordinates followed by eigenvalue monotonicity under
Loewner order.  Hence a known, uniformly well-conditioned dense representation
preserves both exact risk and the marginal spectral exponent.  The
experiments below also test an ambient minimum-norm learner that is not given
the latent decoder.
