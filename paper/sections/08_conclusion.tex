\section{Conclusion}

We separated the marginal spectrum of a prediction task from the innovation
spectrum of a persistent stream.  Their mismatch yields an exact minimax law in
which mode-dependent persistence changes the data exponent, while uniform
persistence changes only constants.  A matched-integrated-autocorrelation
construction shows directly why one global effective sample size can miss the
slope, and the resulting compute law shifts optimal resources from model size
toward innovation diversity.  Raw-horizon, heavy-tail, noise, dense
representation, and negative-control results identify both the reach and the
boundary of the mechanism.  The broader lesson is that scaling with dependent
data is governed by how quickly new predictive directions arrive, not only by
how many correlated rows are logged.
