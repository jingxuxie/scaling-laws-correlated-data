\section{From Innovations to Raw Trajectories}
\label{sec:raw-horizon}

The exact identity counts completed innovations, whereas a logged dataset is
usually specified by its number of raw observations.  We therefore concatenate
integer-duration blocks and stop after exactly $N$ examples.  In the noiseless
case, seeing any part of a block reveals its spectral coordinate, so let
$\widehat\theta^{(N)}$ memorize every modeled mode encountered by time $N$.
The number of renewals before $N$ may fluctuate strongly when dwell times are
heavy tailed; our proof avoids replacing it by its expectation.

\begin{theorem}[Raw horizon and inspection paradox]
\label{thm:raw}
Assume Equation~\eqref{eq:power-laws}, $\sigma=0$, and
$1<b<a+r+1$.  If the stream starts at a renewal boundary, then for every
$r\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\E\risk(\widehat\theta^{(N)},\theta)
\asympconst M^{-(b-1)}+N^{-(b-1)/(a+r)}.
\label{eq:raw-boundary}
\end{equation}
No finite-variance dwell assumption is needed.  Under stationary equilibrium
initialization, for $r>0$,
\begin{align}
\E\risk(\widehat\theta^{(N)}_{\rm eq},\theta)
\asympconst{}&M^{-(b-1)}+N^{-(b-1)/(a+r)}\nonumber\\
&+N^{-(a-1)/r}.
\label{eq:raw-stationary}
\end{align}
For $r=0$, the final term is absent.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Mode-wise hitting times.}
For mode $j$, let $H_j$ be the raw start time of its first block.  The number
of failed innovations before the first $j$-block is geometric.  Conditioning
the dwell distribution on failure gives the elementary sandwich
\begin{align}
\E H_j&=\mu/q_j-\tau_j\le\mu/q_j,\nonumber\\
(1-q_j)^N&\le\Prob(H_j\ge N)
\le\min\{1,\mu/(Nq_j)\}.
\label{eq:hitting-sandwich}
\end{align}
The upper bound is Markov's inequality applied separately to each mode; the
lower bound observes that at most $N$ blocks can start in $N$ raw steps.
Summing the sandwich against $s_j$ at the critical index
$j\asymp N^{1/(a+r)}$ proves Equation~\eqref{eq:raw-boundary}.  This argument is
stronger than concentrating the total renewal count: it remains valid even
when the duration variance is infinite.

\paragraph{Why stationarity is different.}
At equilibrium, the block containing time zero is selected in proportion to
its length, so its mode has law $\lambda$ rather than $q$.  Conditional on
mode $j$, its forward residual life $R$ is uniform on
$\{1,\ldots,\tau_j\}$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\Prob(R\ge N)\asympconst N^{-(a-1)/r}.
\label{eq:residual-tail-main}
\end{equation}
On the event $R\ge N$, no refresh occurs in the entire observation window.
This creates the additional term in Equation~\eqref{eq:raw-stationary}.  The
overall stationary slope is therefore the slower of the innovation exponent
$(b-1)/(a+r)$ and the residual-life exponent $(a-1)/r$.  In particular, the
starting convention is not merely a finite-sample detail for heavy dwell
profiles.

\paragraph{Noisy horizons from fractional moments.}
Noise can also be transferred without requiring $\E\tau_J^2<\infty$.  Let
$D=\tau_J$ for $J\sim q$ and $\beta=(b-1)/(a+r)$.

\begin{theorem}[Noisy raw horizons from fractional moments]
\label{thm:noisy-raw}
Let $1<b\le a+r$, $\sigma=O(1)$, and restrict estimates to the first $M$
modes.  Set $B_N=\lfloor N/(2\mu)\rfloor$.  If $r=0$, or if
\begin{equation}
\frac{b-1}{a+r}<\frac{a-1}{r},
\label{eq:fractional-condition}
\end{equation}
then, over the signed class $\Theta_s$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:minimax},
\begin{equation}
\inf_{\widetilde\theta}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_s}
\E\risk(\widetilde\theta,\theta)
\asympconst M^{-(b-1)}+N^{-\beta}.
\label{eq:noisy-raw-rate}
\end{equation}
The upper bound uses the first $B_N$ completed blocks when they fit in the
horizon and outputs zero otherwise.
\end{theorem}

For $r>0$, $\E D^\nu<\infty$ exactly when
$\nu<1+(a-1)/r$.  Under Equation~\eqref{eq:fractional-condition}, choose
$1+\beta<\nu\le2$ below this threshold.  The von Bahr--Esseen inequality gives
\begin{equation}
\Prob\!\left(\sum_{k\le B_N}D_k>N\right)
=O(N^{1-\nu})=o(N^{-\beta}).
\label{eq:completion-probability}
\end{equation}
On the completion event, Theorem~\ref{thm:scaling} applies with
$B_N\asymp N$; its variance term is absorbed when $b\le a+r$.  The unseen-sign
prior gives the matching lower bound.  The result strictly extends the
finite-variance range $r<a-1$ and separates the moment condition needed for
noisy averaging from the weaker condition needed for noiseless coverage.
