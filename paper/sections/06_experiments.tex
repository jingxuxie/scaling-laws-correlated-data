\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

All calculations use direct summation or lightweight Monte Carlo and run on a
laptop.  Code, seeds, exact CSV/JSON outputs, and deterministic tests are
included.  Slopes are fit only on the largest scales specified before
inspection; local slopes and standard errors are retained in the repository.

\paragraph{Exact, raw-horizon, and matched-ESS rates.}
For $(a,b)=(2,1.8)$ and $r\in\{0,0.4,0.8\}$, exact finite-$B$ slopes are
$-0.399999$, $-0.333333$, and $-0.285714$, versus predictions $-0.4$, $-1/3$,
and $-2/7$.  Independent block Monte Carlo differs from the exact identity by
at most 2.1\%.  Streams stopped after exactly $N$ raw examples give
$-0.399$, $-0.339$, and $-0.290$.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:matched-iat}, we set $a=2$, $r=0.8$, and calibrate
both processes to trace IAT 15.  Three hundred stationary trajectories per
scale give exponent $0.401$ under uniform persistence and $0.284$ under aligned
persistence, versus $0.400$ and $0.286$.  Because the global IAT is identical,
this experiment rules out a constant horizontal rescaling as an explanation
of the gap.

In an infinite-variance setting $(a,b,r)=(2,5.5,3)$, the boundary-start slope
is $-0.895$ versus $-0.9$; stationary initialization slows it to $-0.346$,
close to the inspection prediction $-1/3$.  The directly evaluated
forward-recurrence tail has fitted exponent $0.332$.

\paragraph{Noise, model choice, and compute allocation.}
Exact evaluation of the reciprocal-binomial term confirms both noise regimes.
For a hard target $(a,b,r)=(2,1.8,0.8)$, the fitted risk and frontier-model
exponents are $0.284$ and $0.354$ versus $2/7$ and $1/2.8$.  For a smooth
target $b=4$, optimizing over $M$ gives exponents $0.765$ for risk and $0.262$
for model size, versus $3/4$ and $1/4$.

We also minimize the exact noiseless risk under integer budgets $C=MB$.  For
$r=0,0.4,0.8$, fitted compute exponents are $0.2666$, $0.2352$, and $0.2104$,
matching $(b-1)/(a+r+1)=0.2667,0.2353,0.2105$.  The fitted model exponents are
$0.329,0.295,0.263$, while the innovation exponents are
$0.671,0.705,0.737$, confirming the allocation shift in
Corollary~\ref{cor:compute-optimal}.

\paragraph{Dense representation and negative control.}
We rotate 512 latent modes into dense observed vectors.  Exact decoding agrees
with latent coverage risk within 1.4\% Monte Carlo error.  A perturbed
dictionary with condition number 4.67 and maximum row coherence 0.13 is
deliberately more difficult: ambient minimum-norm fits give slopes $-0.398$
and $-0.275$ for $r=0$ and $0.8$, versus $-0.400$ and $-0.286$.  This tests a
learner that is not supplied with the inverse representation used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:dense}.

By contrast, in the tested dense Gaussian AR regime, batch-regression slopes
are $0.92$--$1.12$, far from renewal predictions $0.27$--$0.40$.  In finite
dimension, such Gaussian designs have full column rank almost surely once
$N\ge D$, hence noiseless least squares recovers exactly.  The control is
therefore a deliberate attempt to falsify an overbroad interpretation: the
renewal exponent is not a generic consequence of autocorrelation.

\paragraph{Sequential appliance-energy data.}
We use the 10-minute appliance-energy stream of
\citet{candanedo2017appliances}.  PCA gives empirical marginal masses
$\widehat\lambda_j$, ridge coefficients give target energies
$\widehat s_j$, and each score has estimated integrated autocorrelation time
$\widehat\tau_j$ using the initial-positive-sequence estimator
\cite{geyer1992practical}.  We fit only an amplitude and scale on the first
four sizes (128 through 1024), then extrapolate to the next three sizes (2048
through 8192) with
\begin{equation}
G_\rho(N)=\sum_j\widehat s_j e^{-cN\rho_j},
\qquad
\rho_j\in\left\{\widehat\lambda_j,
\widehat\lambda_j/\widehat\tau_j\right\}.
\label{eq:real-proxy}
\end{equation}
For contiguous windows, out-of-range log-RMSE falls from 1.099 to 0.951
(13.4\%).  For random subsets, the spatial proxy is better (0.823 versus
1.299), as expected when sampling destroys temporal persistence.  The latter
is a falsification control, not an inconvenient ablation: a genuinely temporal
correction should lose its advantage after order is randomized.  This real
experiment is an illustrative diagnostic rather than a direct test of the
renewal generative model.
