\begin{abstract}
Scaling-law theories usually assume independent examples, while correlated
training data are often summarized by one effective sample size. We identify a
regime where this scalar correction fails: persistence aligned with weak
spectral directions changes the learning exponent. We introduce \emph{spectral
renewal regression}. Mode $j$ has marginal covariance mass
$\lambda_j\asymp j^{-a}$, dwell time $\tau_j\asymp j^r$, and target energy
$s_j=\lambda_j\theta_j^2\asymp j^{-b}$, but statistically new blocks arrive
with probability $q_j\propto\lambda_j/\tau_j\asymp j^{-(a+r)}$. For an
$M$-mode estimator trained on $B$ innovations, we derive an exact finite-sample
risk identity and prove
\begin{align*}
\E\risk=\Theta\big(&M^{-(b-1)}+B^{-(b-1)/(a+r)}\\
&+\sigma^2B^{-1}\min\{M,B^{1/(a+r)}\}\big).
\end{align*}
The noiseless expression is the exact minimax risk over a signed source class.
For exactly $N$ raw observations, the same boundary-start exponent holds for
all dwell-tail regimes; stationary initialization adds an
inspection-paradox term $N^{-(a-1)/r}$. A fractional-moment argument further
transfers the noisy hard-target rate to raw horizons even when dwell times have
infinite variance. We prove invariance under well-conditioned dense changes of
representation and construct stationary nonseparable Markov realizations.
Exact summation, renewal Monte Carlo, dense-dictionary regression, and a dense
Gaussian autoregressive negative control separate the renewal mechanism from
correlation alone. On appliance-energy data, a persistence-adjusted spectral
proxy reduces contiguous-window extrapolation log-RMSE by 13.4\% relative to a
spatial-only proxy, while the advantage disappears under randomized sampling.
\end{abstract}
