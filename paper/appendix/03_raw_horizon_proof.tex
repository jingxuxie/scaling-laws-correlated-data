\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:raw-horizon}}
\label{app:raw-horizon}

Let $L_k:=\tau_{J_k}$ and $A_m:=\sum_{k=1}^mL_k$. The variables $(L_k)$ are
i.i.d. with mean $\mu$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\E L_1^2
=\sum_jq_j\tau_j^2
=\frac1Z\sum_j\lambda_j\tau_j
\asympconst\sum_j j^{-(a-r)}<\infty
\label{eq:duration-second-moment}
\end{equation}
precisely when $r<a-1$. Write $v:=\operatorname{Var}(L_1)$ and
$S_0:=\sum_js_j<\infty$.

For a realized innovation sequence, define its noiseless missing-energy risk
after $m$ blocks by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{m,M}
:=\sum_{j>M}s_j
+\sum_{j\le M}s_j
\1\{j\notin\{J_1,\ldots,J_m\}\}.
\label{eq:pathwise-missing-risk}
\end{equation}
This quantity is nonincreasing in $m$, is bounded by $S_0$, and satisfies
$\E\mathcal L_{m,M}$ equal to the right side of
Equation~\eqref{eq:minimax}. Since one noiseless raw observation reveals the
current signed coordinate, the fixed-horizon estimator has pathwise risk
$\mathcal L_{T_N,M}$.

For the upper bound, let $B_-:=\lfloor N/(2\mu)\rfloor$, with the finitely many
values for which $B_-<1$ absorbed into constants. Monotonicity gives
\begin{equation}
\E\mathcal L_{T_N,M}
\le \E\mathcal L_{B_-,M}
+S_0\Prob(T_N<B_-).
\label{eq:raw-upper-sandwich}
\end{equation}
The event $T_N<B_-$ implies $A_{B_-}\ge N$. Since
$\E A_{B_-}=\mu B_-\le N/2$, Chebyshev's inequality yields
\begin{equation}
\Prob(T_N<B_-)
\le\Prob(A_{B_-}-\E A_{B_-}\ge N/2)
\le\frac{4vB_-}{N^2}
=O(N^{-1}).
\label{eq:few-renewals}
\end{equation}

For the lower bound, take $B_+:=\lceil2N/\mu\rceil$. Again by monotonicity,
\begin{equation}
\E\mathcal L_{T_N,M}
\ge \E\mathcal L_{B_+,M}
-S_0\Prob(T_N>B_+).
\label{eq:raw-lower-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Now $T_N>B_+$ implies $A_{B_+}<N$. Let
$\phi(t):=\E e^{-tL_1}$. Finite mean gives
$\log\phi(t)=-\mu t+o(t)$ as $t\downarrow0$, so for some fixed small $t>0$,
$\log\phi(t)\le-3\mu t/4$. Chernoff's bound then gives
\begin{equation}
\Prob(T_N>B_+)
\le e^{tN}\phi(t)^{B_+}
\le e^{-tN/2}.
\label{eq:many-renewals}
\end{equation}

Both $B_-$ and $B_+$ are constant multiples of $N$. Applying the noiseless
part of Theorem~\ref{thm:scaling} to
Equations~\eqref{eq:raw-upper-sandwich} and
\eqref{eq:raw-lower-sandwich} gives the desired rate, because
$\beta:=(b-1)/(a+r)<1$ under
Equation~\eqref{eq:raw-horizon-conditions}, and hence the $O(N^{-1})$ and
exponential errors are negligible relative to $N^{-\beta}$.

For equilibrium initialization, the initial forward recurrence time has finite
mean whenever $\E L_1^2<\infty$. Its probability of exceeding $N/2$ is therefore
$O(N^{-1})$. Conditional on a shorter initial residual, the remaining stream is
an ordinary renewal process of length at least $N/2$, so the upper sandwich
applies with different constants. For the lower bound, the equilibrium block
adds one mode $J_0\sim\lambda$ before the ordinary renewal sequence. Its expected
reduction of the $B$-block missing energy is at most
\begin{equation}
\sum_j s_j\lambda_j(1-q_j)^B
\le C\sum_j j^{-(a+b)}e^{-cBj^{-(a+r)}}
=O\!\left(B^{-(a+b-1)/(a+r)}\right),
\label{eq:equilibrium-initial-mode}
\end{equation}
which is $o(B^{-(b-1)/(a+r)})$. Combining this term with the same exponential
lower-tail bound for subsequent renewals proves the stationary version.
