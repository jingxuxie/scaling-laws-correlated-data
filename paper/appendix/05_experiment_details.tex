\section{Additional Experimental Details}
\label{app:experiments}

The exact experiment uses
\[
\lambda_j=\frac{j^{-a}}{\zeta(a)},
\quad
q_j=\frac{j^{-(a+r)}}{\zeta(a+r)},
\quad
s_j=\frac{j^{-b}}{\zeta(b)}.
\]
The infinite model tail is computed by the Hurwitz zeta function. The leading
infinite-model asymptotic constant used as a numerical check is
\begin{equation}
C_{a,b,r}
=\frac{1}{\zeta(b)(a+r)}
\zeta(a+r)^{(b-1)/(a+r)}
\Gamma\!\left(\frac{b-1}{a+r}\right).
\end{equation}

Figure~\ref{fig:mc} samples the infinite Zipf law directly. Each marker is the
mean conditional risk over 400 independently sampled datasets. The solid
curves are the exact finite-$B$ expression, not asymptotic fits.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{monte_carlo_validation.pdf}
\caption{Monte Carlo samples of the persistent block process agree with the
exact risk identity.}
\label{fig:mc}
\end{figure}

For the raw-horizon experiment, deterministic integer dwell times are
$\ell_j=\lceil j^r\rceil$ and innovations are sampled exactly from
$q_j\propto j^{-a}/\ell_j$ by rejection sampling from a Zipf$(a+r)$ proposal.
Each trajectory stops when its accumulated duration first reaches $N$; the
partially observed final block counts as touched. Figure~\ref{fig:raw-horizon}
shows that random stopping changes finite-scale constants but not the predicted
slopes in the finite-variance regime.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{raw_horizon_validation.pdf}
\caption{Monte Carlo validation at a fixed raw trajectory length. Error bars
show standard errors over 250 independent trajectories.}
\label{fig:raw-horizon}
\end{figure}

The repository contains deterministic seeds, CSV outputs, unit tests, and a
single command that regenerates all figures. The tests check normalization,
monotonicity in $B$ and $M$, asymptotic slope recovery, recovery of total prior
energy at $B=0$, Monte Carlo agreement, and increased risk under label noise.
